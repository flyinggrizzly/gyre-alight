# Creating a character

Before the adventure starts, we will fill out most of our Character's sheet.

## Identity

Give them a **name**, **Belief**, and **Instinct**.

- **Beliefs** will be tested, and will earn your Character Fate Points when you play them in accordance with their Belief, or when it shatters.
- **Instincts** are macros that allow your Character to break the normal rules of the game. When you benefit from them,
  or when you let them cause you problems, you'll earn Fate Points.

## Traits

Next, give them 2 **Traits**. Traits are things like "Ugly as Sin", or "Cunning", or "Chatterbox". They describe your
character (you might be ugly, but if you're Ugly as Sin, you're the ugliest person anyone you meet has ever seen), **and
they also can give you an edge in Tests, or help you earn Checks to recover in Camp.**

## Wises

Now choose 2 **Wises**. Wises are specialist knowledge or wisdom that set your Character apart, and can help them and
their friends with bonus dice, and chances to avoid failure.

## Choose Skills

Assign 8D amongst Skills of your choice. No Skill can exceed a rating of 3.

## Assign your Attributes

Next, assign your Attributes, with a 3, 2, and 1 distributed randomly, or as you choose.

Attributes are **Wit, Power, Grit**.

For the Attribute at 3, distribute 2 more dice among skills rooted in it. They may not exceed 2

**If you distribute them randomly, choose 1 boon**:

- take 1 extra Trait
- increase an existing Trait to level 2
- choose 1 extra Wise
- increase once Skill from 2 to 3D
- assign 2 extra Skill Dice (but no skill may exceed 2)

## Note your Might and Precedence

Your Might and Precedence are set by your level.

Might is your strength relative to the world around you. You'll start at Might 3, and are stronger than most folk.
Things like Orcs are your equal, Trolls have some Might on you, Ogres can really thrash you, and Giants will flatten
you.

Precedence is your social standing. Adventurers are outcasts. You are Precedence 0. Your peers are drunks and vagrants,
and most folks look at you askance.

See _Torchbearer 2e_ for more explanation.

## Note your Resources and Circles

Resources is an abstracted stat that represents your wealth, and ability to access the things you need, pay your bills,
etc. It starts at 1, and advances like your other skills.

Circles is your ability to find people to help you. It also starts at 1, and advances like other skills.

See _Torchbearer 2e_ for details.

## Details on Beliefs, Instincts, Traits, Wises, Attributes, and Skills

### Attributes

Your Attributes are Power, Wit, and Grit.

Power underpins anything you do that is root in your physical strenght and endurance.

Wit underlies any action where your sharpness of mind is critical.

Grit carries you through the most trying of moments, where your patience, is tried, where the only way through is to
grit your teeth and bear it.

### Skills

Now choose **4 or 5 Skills**, with 8 Dice (or 10 if you assigned your Attributes randomly and didn't take an extra
Trait) distributed between them. No skill should have a score greater than 2D at creation.

## Fate Points

Instead of experience or stress, we use Fate Points. You earn these for roleplaying, good planning, and making the game
fun for the rest of the table.

## Skills

Skills are the many and various things your Character is good at. They're both broad and narrow in weird ways. Each
skill lists its Root Attribute in parenthesis after its name, like `Alchemy (Wit)`.

## Beliefs

Beliefs are part of what defines your character, and helps us understand their outlook on the world. It also helps the
GM understand _what you, as a player, and hoping to see come up in play._

If your Belief is _"It is unconscionable to leave the weak undefended"_, you better believe that the GM is going to slap
weaker people into your game, and make you choose between succeeding in your mission, and protecting them. (**Note to
GMs: heckin' do this!**)

### Fate from Beliefs

At the end of each session, check the following:

- If your Belief came up in a meaningful way in play, earn 1 Fate
- If your Belief was **changed** in a scene, rewrite it and earn 2 Fate

## Instincts

Instincts are "macros", phrased like _"if ..., then I ..."_, or _"when ..., then ..."_, or _"always ..."_.

When the GM is making a Move, your Character usually won't have time to react. Or, if you didn't say _"I'm boiling the
water from that spring to make sure it's safe"_, if it was fouled, it's too late to address the issue once your
Character has drunk it.

With an Instinct though, this all changes. If your Instinct is _"Always make sure the food and water are safe"_, you
don't have to say it in play anymore, **and** if you have to make a Test to do it, it doesn't tick Momentum.

Another example: if the GM is making a Move, and your camp is interrupted, but you have the instict _"never be
unarmed"_, while the other Characters might have to scramble for a blade or risk fighting unarmed, your sword is already
in your hand.

Instincts should be specific. An Instinct that is too broad feels cheap, means we don't actually learn anything about
your Character, and makes the game less fun. Make sure they're only situationally useful, not globally.

### Fate from Instincts

At the end of each session, check the following:

- If your Instinct grants you the chance to make a Test during play, you should earn a Fate for it. If it happens more than once, earn two Fate (but not more).
- If you let your Instinct get you in trouble (for example _"when I'm surprised, draw my blade"_, and you do so when approached by the Duke... well), you earn 2 Fate for it.

## Traits

Traits are things that are unique and remarkable about your Character. They should be one or a few words, always a
single concept (consider: if German would slam a few words together into a new noun, your few-word trait is probably
OK). They can be used to grant yourself bonuce Dice, or even successes, on rolls where they are fictionally applicable.

### Invoking Traits to benefit yourself

If your Trait is germane to the Test at hand, you get:

- **Level 1:** +1D once per session
- **Level 2:** +1D twice per session
- **Level 3:** +1s to any tied or successful roll

### Invoking Traits to harm yourself

**Once per session** you can use a Trait against yourself:

| Situation | Effect | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Independent Test | You get -1D | 1 Check |
| Versus Test | Opponent gets +2D | 2 Checks |
| Tied Versus Test | Break the tie in your opponent's favor | 2 Checks |


### Fate from Traits

At the end of each session, check the following:

- If you let your Trait get you into trouble, earn one Fate. Example: I'm **Honest to a Fault**, and I tell the merchant my colleague is haggling with that the treasure is actually just some foolsgold we found on the path. Now we get no money.

## Wises

A Wise is specialist knowledge or experience your Character has. You can invoke them to help yourself or others in a few
ways:

- **Aiding a friend:** if your Wise is relevant to _someone else's_ Test, you may grant them +1D in Aid. Aiding insulates you from Condition fallout, but not Twists. You can't both Help and Aid the same test, it's one or the other.
- **Deeper Understanding:** you can invoke your Wise and spend 1 Fate to reroll any failed die on a Test. Explain how the Wise helps you.
- **Of Course!:** invoke your Wise and spend 2 Fate to reroll **all** failed dice on a Test. Explain how your knowledge and wisdome allow you to see a hidden trap, and may help you avoid it!
