# Playing the game

Play proceeds in a conversation, between the Players and the GM, about what the Characters do, how the world responds,
and how they Characters overcome. When the outcome of something isn't certain, we roll the dice in a Test.

## Scenes and Tests

Don't make every little thing a Test. Tests should be reserved for when we are interested in the dramatic outcome of
something--what will the Characters do if they can't reach the portcullis before nightfall? can the Fallen Hero overcome
That Voice long enough to save the village?

Each of these is played out in a Scene. Some Scenes flow directly from one into another, and others are more disparate,
separated by time. But each Scene should be answering some question where both success and failure are interesting.

Most Scenes should culminate in a Test.

**Each Test, success or fail, will tick Momentum ever upward.** #PTQ(Or do we only tick up on successes?)

### Making a Test

When it's time to roll the dice, do the following:

1. Describe how your Character is pursuing their desire
2. State your desired outcome or intent ("I scare the goblins away", "I filch the gold pouch on his belt unnoticed", or "I convince the baron to send a knight to our hamlet to protect us from the ghouls")
3. the GM will Choose a Skill for you to Test, and then tell you the Obstacle, which you must meet or exceed
4. Assemble your dice pool
    - If you have the necessary Skill, add its rating to its Root Attribute rating, otherwise roll Beginner's Luck
    - Maybe add a Trait die
    - Maybe add a Gear die
    - And maybe a Supply die
    - Ask for Help or Aid
    - Maybe add Momentum dice
5. Roll your dice. Any dice showing **4, 5, or 6 are successes**. Any dice showing **1, 2, or 3 are traitors.**
6. Assess the result. **If you have successes equal to or exceeding the Obstacle, you succeed.**
  - For a Versus Test, you need to break a tie if you didn't exceed your opponent's successes
6. Spend any after-roll Fate to change failure into success:
  - Invoke a Wise with 1 or 2 Fate to reroll some or all of the traitors
    - Any traitors in the Momentum dice are still granted to the GM for their evil ends #PTQ(Or do we take them away from the GM?)
  - Spend 1 Fate to explode all dice showing a 6. Gather dice equal to the number of 6es in your first roll, and roll them, adding any new successes to your total.
    - Do this **after** you reroll traitors with your Wise

#### Versus Tests

Some Tests are directly opposed by other actors in the world--these are Versus Tests.

When making a Versus Test, both you and the GM roll, and your opponent's result sets your Obstacle, **which you must
beat.**

If there is a tie, you have a choice:

- call on a Level 3 Trait to break the tie in your favor
- if your Precedence or Might is greater, it grants you +1s and breaks the tie in your favor
- you can invoke a Trait **against** yourself and break the tie in the GM's favor, and earn 2 Checks for Camp
- you can go to a tiebreaker roll

##### Tiebreaker Rolls

If you rolled a Skill, use its root to break the tie. Your opponent will do the same.

If you rolled an Attribute directly (for Beginner's Luck or any other reason), reroll it.

Remember, you can always add Momentum to your rolls.

#### Failure

If you fail to meet or exceed the Obstacle of your Test, the GM will make a **Twist**, or give you a **Harm**.

**Twists** are new obstacles or information that makes your life harder. It might be something you hadn't realized
standing in your way of achieving your goal, or that you have a new complication. In some cases, these will be hard
failures, but these cases should be rare.

**Harm** is expected in your adventuring trade, and most of it is manageable. But I hope you brought bandages and
splints with you. When taking Harm, you should still get what you were after most of the time.

### Good Ideas

If the players have a particularly Good Idea about how they are acheiving their goal, give them what they want without a
Test, and without building Momentum.

### Beginner's Luck

Not everyone is an expert in everything, but that doesn't mean you shouldn't try. This is how we learn.

When making a Beginner's Luck Test, you begin as usual, however:

- the dice from your Attribute, Help, Aid, Gear, and Supplies **are halved (rounding up)**
- the dice from Traits, and Momentum are applied **after** the halving

#### Beginner's Luck and Tools of the Trade

Anyone with a Skill is assumed to have access to the necessary tools to practice it (e.g. lockpicks for Crime), so long
as the GM hasn't taken them as part of a Twist.

If you are testing with Beginner's Luck, we assume you **don't** have the necessary tools, **and increase the Obstacle
by 1.**

If one of your friends can loan you the tools, do not increase the Obstacle.

**When a Character does not have the Skill required for a Test, they make a Beginner's Luck Test.**

## Momentum

Momentum is a dangerous thing. When you enter a dangerous situation with it at your back, you have the upper hand, can do
more, reach farther, hit harder...

But equally, should you trip, that Momentum can crush you into the ground even harder than a troll's club.

Be careful.

### Building Momentum

Momentum starts at 0, and increases by one after every successful Test or Conflict.

### Using Momentum

When making a Test, you can add bonus dice equal to the current Momentum to your pool. Use a separate color for these
dice, or roll them separately, so you can see how badly you lost control of your Momentum, and how much of the GM will
bring to bear against you.

**When you Ride your Momentum,** add dice to your pool equal to the Momentum, and...

**If your Momentum is 5 or more, choose 1, if your Momentum is 10 or more, choose 2:**

- Increase your Might or Precedence (may be chosen twice for each)
- If your Test succeeds, reduce Momentum by 1 (costs 2)

### Traitorous Momentum

**When a Momentum die is a traitor, give it to the GM.** These traitors can be rerolled by calling on your Traits and
spending Fate, but **you still give the die to the GM.** Even when you correct, a wobble at speed is dangerous.

The GM can use Traitorous Momentum against you in the following ways:

| | TM Cost | Effect|
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What comes around goes around** | _n_ | Spend any number of Traitorous Momentum dice to add to a Test made by one of your opponents |
| **Myopathy** | 2 | When making a Test, you missed something. The GM spends Traitorous Momentum to inflict a Twist. |
| **Overstretched** | 2 | After a Test, _successful or not_, The Character marks Harm of your choice based on the current momentum level: 0-4: Hesitation; 5-9: Hampered; 10+: Heaving. |
| **Extreme Myopathy** | 3 | At any time, the GM spends Traitorous Momentum to inflict a Twist. |
| **Like a Bat Outta Hell** | 5 | The Character marks Harm of your choice based on the current momentum level: 0-4: Hesitation; 5-9: Hampered; 10+: Heaving. |


## Harm

(Replaces _Torchbearer 2e's_ conditions.)

A Character can take a few knocks, but each one slows them down a little. When you fail a Test, or have to give a
concession to a foe, you will mark a Harm. Consider what circumstances lead you to mark the Harm, and its level,
and come up with a condition for it.


| Level | Power | Wit | Grit | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hesitation |  | | | +1D to opponents in Versus Tests involving the Attribute and derived Skills |
| Hampered | | | | -1D to any Tests involving the Attribute or its derived Skills |
| Heaving | | | | -1s to any Tests involving the Attribute or its derived Skills |


If you have already marked Harm for an Attribute at one level, and need to mark another, **you mark it at the next level
up.**

> #### Example
> I have the Power/Hesitation harm "Sprain", but just failed a Test and need to mark another Power/Hesitation harm.
> Since I already have "Sprain", I instead mark "Fracture" for Power/Hampered level.

In some extreme cases, this might mean you need to mark Heaving Harm when you're asked to mark Hesitation if both it and
Hampered are already marked for an Attribute. 

### Dead Weight

In some extreme cases, or if you can mark no other Harm, you must mark **Dead Weight**. In this state, you may not
attempt any Tests without spending 3 Fate.

If you have marked Dead Weight, and need to mark another Harm, your Character dies.

## Might and Precedence

How you stack up physically and socially against your peers and opponents. See _Torchbearer 2e_ for more details. The
relevant information for this playtest is that:

| Might/Precedence difference | Might Capability, Restriction | Precedence Capability, Restriction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any | You may flee | You may trick and riddle |
| Equal to or less than your own | Drive off, kill, capture | Convince a crowd, Convince an individual, Haggle |
| 1 Greater than your own | Drive off, kill | Convince a crowd, Haggle |
| 2+ Greater than your own | Drive off | Convince a crowd |
